Privacy policy — Dawaa

Last updated: 4 September 2026

Google Play requires every app to publish a privacy policy at a public URL, whether or not it collects anything. This is that document.

The app collects nothing about you automatically. One thing leaves your device, and only when you deliberately ask for it: the message you type into the "Report a problem or request a feature" box. Everything below explains that one exception and the things stored on your own phone.

What is collected automatically

In the app: nothing. There is no account, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting, no tracking, and no server of ours anywhere. The app does not report that you opened it, what you looked up, or where you are.

On the website there is one exception, and it is counted below under "The website". The app and the website are the same reference and are not the same piece of software, and this document is careful to say which is which.

The one thing you can send

The app has a "Report a problem or request a feature" button. If you type a message and tap Send, these things are transmitted, and nothing else:

Your messageExactly the text you typed
A nameOnly if you choose to type one; the field is optional and may be left blank
App versione.g. "1.2.0+7", so a bug can be matched to a build
Which screenThe condition you were viewing, or "general"

It is delivered to a private Google Form belonging to the app's author, so Google processes it as the host of that form. Nothing is transmitted unless you tap Send. If you never use that button, the app transmits nothing, ever.

If the phone has no connection when you tap Send, the message is saved in the app's private storage on your device and re-sent the next time the app opens. It is deleted from the device once it has been sent.

On the website version there is no such queue: tapping Send opens the form itself in a new tab with your message already filled in, and you press Submit there. That is deliberate — a web browser cannot confirm whether the form accepted a silent submission, and the app will not tell you a report was sent when it cannot know.

What is stored, and where

Five things are saved on your own device, in the app's private storage:

FavouritesConditions you starred
Recent lookupsThe last 50 conditions you opened
Unavailable brandsTrade names you marked as "the pharmacy didn't have it"
Prescribed countsHow often you have written each brand
Open countsHow often you have opened each condition, which ranks the "Most used" list
Text sizeThe text size you chose in Settings
Cautions expandedWhether you left the cautions section open or folded
Unsent reportsOnly if a Send failed; deleted once it goes through

All of them are stored only on your device, are readable only by this app, and are deleted when you uninstall it. None is backed up to us, because there is no us to back up to.

Patient data

The app never asks who the patient is. There is no name field, no date of birth, no identifier of any kind. Please do not type patient-identifying details into the report box — it is read by a person, and it is not a medical record.

Health data

The app contains health information — drug doses, guidelines, conditions. It does not collect health information about you or your patients.

Children

The app is a professional reference for prescribers. It is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone.

Permissions

The app requests no runtime permissions: no camera, microphone, location, contacts, or storage. It does declare the Android INTERNET permission, which is what makes the report-a-problem button possible. That permission is not used for anything else — the entire drug database ships inside the app and is read offline.

The website

dawaareference.com serves the same reference as plain web pages. Two things about it differ from the app.

It counts visits, using Cloudflare Web Analytics. Every page loads a small script from Cloudflare that records that the page was viewed. It is cookieless: it stores nothing on your device, sets no identifier, and cannot follow you to another site or recognise you when you come back. What Cloudflare reports to us is a count - which pages were viewed, how many times, roughly where in the world from, which referring site, and which browser and device type - and it is aggregated, never a list of visits and never tied to a person. We use it to know which cards are actually being read. Cloudflare processes it as our provider; their privacy terms govern it.

Your browser reaches our host to fetch the pages. Serving any web page means the host sees the request, including your IP address, the same way every website works. The pages are static files: nothing you type into the search box is sent anywhere, because that search runs entirely inside your own browser.

The app does neither of these things. It has no analytics of any kind and fetches nothing to display a card.

Third parties

Two, and both are named above: Google, as the host of the form a problem report is submitted to, and Cloudflare, as the analytics provider and host for the website. There are no advertising networks and no SDKs that phone home in this version. Your data cannot be sold, because it is not collected.

Changes

If this ever changes, this document will be updated before that version is published, and the change will be described here rather than buried. That is what happened on 24 August 2026: an earlier version of this policy said the app "sends nothing anywhere" and had "no internet permission". That stopped being true when the report-a-problem button shipped, and this document is the correction.

It happened again, and this is that correction too. On 4 September 2026 this policy still said there were "no analytics providers" while the website it is published on was already loading Cloudflare Web Analytics on every page, including this one. The script was added to the website's copy of this page without the text being amended. Nothing about the app changed and nothing new started being collected; what changed is that this document now says what the website was already doing. The section above describes it.
